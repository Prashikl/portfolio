01 Problem

Rich data can still produce poor decisions.

Sellers had to piece together agreements, subscriptions, renewals, concessions, pricing, invoices, proposals, and commercial history from fragmented sources. Left alone, the product would have become a set of disconnected reports shaped by backend structure, with redundant history, unclear naming, and gaps in the data.

My role

I helped reframe the product, explored search and information architecture, challenged low-value or unsafe detail, partnered on seller validation, and shaped the agreement, invoice, pricing, proposal, history, renewal, and concession patterns.

02 System map

Start with seller questions, not the database.

I mapped customer, billing account, billing profile, agreements, subscriptions, proposals, orders, invoices, amendments, and renewals, then built the hierarchy around three questions: what does the customer have, what needs attention, and what can the seller do next?

Orient

Stable customer context

One traversal anchor keeps the result model consistent, whichever way a seller searches.

Trust

Source it, don't silently copy

Use authoritative platform data, with deep links where local detail is incomplete.

Continue

Keep migration legible

Preserve agreement transition context while hiding or phasing low-value history.

Return

Remember recent context

Sellers get back to up to ten recently entered billing accounts, saved per user.

03 Decisions

Information architecture became product strategy.

Single pane

Orientation, not one giant page

Keep the hierarchy, filters, disclosure, and authoritative exits.

Invoices

Separate from agreement history

Billing-cycle and profile relationships carry different intent and governance.

Billing

Account first, profile available

Sellers find invoices without profile knowledge, and still see the profile for payment context.

History

Keep, hide, phase, or deep-link

Showing legacy data is not a reason to duplicate confusing or incomplete data.

Scope

Clear initial boundaries

The first release limited cloud and channel scope and kept every dependency explicit.

Research

Validate with sellers

Seller questions, prototypes, selective rollout, and telemetry guide each decision.

A coherent product can span several surfaces when users keep their orientation and know which source to trust.

04 Delivery

UX completeness sat on the critical path.

19 May 2026

Specifications and UX walkthroughs were complete for three priority fundamentals, ready for engineering review and estimation.

Readiness gate

Engineering needed complete mocks, screens, controls, and functional specifications before development review, so design readiness gated estimation.

Implementation

Search definitions, source entities, modern and classic behavior, filters, recent accounts, and agreement-transition logic all got worked through with engineering.

05 Outcome

A clear direction the whole team could build toward.

The team now shares a product narrative built around seller decisions and foundational capabilities, along with concrete IA choices for agreements, invoices, billing profiles, renewals, history, and source-system links.

  • Trust and decision value come before visual sophistication.
  • Research should shape the roadmap structure, not approve a nearly final layout.
  • Pair every UX decision with its source entity, capability owner, readiness gate, test case, and production signal.
  • Measure search success, agreement comprehension, renewal actionability, invoice discovery, tool exits, and incidents separately.
