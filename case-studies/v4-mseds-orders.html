Self-service, built on control.

Simplifying complex enterprise ordering and reconciliation while preserving pricing clarity, compliance, validation, collaboration, and recovery.

01 Context

Different order intents carry different truths.

New, anniversary, mid-term, reservation, and reconciliation journeys involve prior quantities, pending actions, product identifiers, pricing, compliance, and different correctness conditions. Manual processing would not scale, but full self-service could not hide safeguards.

Verified contribution

I brought ordering expertise into Phase 1 requirements and owned end-to-end entry, order intent, pending actions, product grids, filters, identifiers, quantities, validation, and guidance. Programme value, launch, and GA decisions remained cross-functional.

02 Evidence

Separate direct evidence from adjacent research.

Direct review evidence showed the need for a simple purchase-or-reservation entry, contextual pending actions, clear compliance logic, meaningful identifiers, familiar filters, and understandable reconciliation quantities. Later UAT exposed broader reservations, future-billing, and save-as-draft gaps.

Adjacent research with three enterprise customer organizations found demand for prefill with editability, guidance, concise notifications, clear roles, collaboration, and control. The PDF explicitly says not to present that study as direct ordering research until its linkage is confirmed.

Self-service is confidence, control, validation, collaboration, and recovery, not the removal of support.
03 Decisions

Keep the entry simple and the consequence local.

Context

Pending actions, not blanket blocks

Keep entry focused while presenting unresolved actions as relevant context.

Coherence

Align filters and grids

Reuse adjacent product behavior to lower relearning and inconsistency.

Guidance

Explain quantities in place

Put reconciliation guidance beside the decision it affects.

Recognition

Use customer-meaningful IDs

Backend precision is not enough when identifiers are poor recognition cues.

Scope

Descoped Profiles

Compliance and complexity risk outweighed near-term value in one phase.

Ownership

Classify before redesign

Separate UX gaps from data, policy, and engineering causes.

04 Readiness

In-flight work needs honest gates.

Design trace

Three August 2025 actions assigned to me—pending-action simplification, filter alignment, and quantity messaging—were marked complete.

UAT fact

UAT identified 35 experience gaps. I was assigned UX classification, design updates, stakeholder walkthroughs, and closure drive; non-UX causes had other owners.

Programme target

The plan proposed 13% lower cost per direct enrollment, a controlled pilot, fifteen enrollments across three regions, and staggered onboarding. These are targets, not realized outcomes.

Status

GA scope was still under assessment in September 2026; fast-follow items and actual pilot participation remained open.

Planned measures included completion, time, abandonment, errors, support contacts, reconciliation success, defects, and confidence segmented by order type.

05 Learning

Responsible phasing is part of the experience.

  • Prefill accelerates only when customers can verify, edit, and understand its source.
  • Segment measurement by scenario; aggregate success can hide difficult paths.
  • Contributing to requirements early removes more churn than polishing late-stage flows.
  • Make go/no-go decisions, residual risk, and defect burn-down visible.
  • Treat accessibility as a required gate; a completed assessment is not yet evidenced.
Open validation

Scenario-level launch dates, research linkage, source review record, “days to minutes” baseline, telemetry, support trends, and original leadership recognition.
