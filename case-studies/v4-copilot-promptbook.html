01 Premise

AI should accelerate sense-making, not outsource judgement.

Augmented design (AX) means designing with AI in the loop, and designing the experiences AI powers. Dense specifications, research, seller evidence, business rules, and meeting decisions created translation loss between research, design, and implementation. I used approved AI-assisted tools to make that material reusable, explore more alternatives, reveal edge states, and give PM and engineering working behavior to inspect earlier.

What I did

Copilot, Copilot Notebooks, Markdown workflows, VS Code, and Claude for synthesis, IA and flow exploration, copy refinement, gap identification, and scenario building, plus interactive prototypes for Commerce Insights and a second commerce product. Engineering kept production ownership, and the prototypes sharpened the conversation.

02 Workflow

Build the scaffolding around the model.

01

Prepare approved evidence

Scope specifications, research synthesis, JTBDs, seller evidence, decisions, screenshots, current code, and business rules.

02

Structure reusable context

Turn fragmented material into notebooks, Markdown context, action records, and stable naming.

03

Explore before converging

Compare IA, flows, content, edge cases, error states, and prototype plans without inventing product logic.

04

Build working behavior

Use an AI-assisted coding environment and fictional data to test interactive alternatives, not just static frames.

05

Inspect and review

Check component reuse, states, responsiveness, accessibility, assumptions, and business rules with PM, research, design, and engineering.

06

Hand off with context

Share the approved prototype, decision record, context, acceptance criteria, and known gaps, so production starts from a shared understanding.

03 Prompt design

Treat a prompt as an interaction contract.

Intent

Define entry conditions

Name persona, job, trigger, available context, and desired outcome; ask when the goal is missing.

Hierarchy

Shape the response

Specify priority, sections, confidence labels, and evidence links; separate facts from hypotheses.

Status

Represent system state

Expose loading, tools, partial completion, routing, failure, and long-running work honestly.

Safety

Constrain and recover

Bound sources and tools; support retry, refine, edit, fallback, escalation, and preserved work.

Reuse

Version stable guidance

Distinguish maintained instructions and domain references from temporary conversation state.

Evaluation

Test scenarios, not vibes

Use golden prompts, variants, edge cases, adversarial asks, expected output, and rubrics.

04 Trust + quality

Human gates turn speed into accountable progress.

I review generated work against source evidence, user intent, product rules, and design standards. Sensitive data stays out of unapproved tools, and consequential actions require consent and review. The wider quality model covers groundedness, task completeness, routing, retrieval, schema correctness, regression, latency, trust and control, accessibility, adoption, and safe failure. Deal Agent research shows why it matters: sellers lost trust when deal-book data, concessions, or rationale looked inaccurate or opaque. Trust is a workflow, not a disclaimer.

My principles

Human accountability, source verification, approved tools, explicit uncertainty, and business-rule checks.

Team controls

Sequential review, PM, research, design, and engineering checks, plus production UAT, security, accessibility, responsible-AI, and release gates.

06 What's next

From assistance to agent experience.

My AI practice has moved from learning and copy and handoff assistance, to structured context pipelines and prototypes, to scenario, orchestration, and quality thinking. Next up: making that practice traceable and shareable, with each artefact linked to its owner, version, source permissions, and evaluation result.

The design problem is not what a model can generate. It is what conditions make its behavior useful, inspectable, and responsible.
